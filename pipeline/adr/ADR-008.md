# ADR-008 — Policy Outcome Integrity & Policy-Certainty Display

**Resolves:** OI-REQ-09 · **Status:** ACCEPTED · **Drives:** F-17, F-08

## Context

Revision 1's scope was narrowly "how to compute a policy-certainty
confidence-display dimension," and assumed the policy outcome space was
effectively binary (`ALLOW` vs. `REQUIRE_APPROVAL`) for that purpose.
Review correctly identified that this framing risks a fact that must never
be in question: spec §16's policy outcome space is **three-valued —
`ALLOW | REQUIRE_APPROVAL | DENY`** — and `DENY` must remain first-class,
fail-closed, and in specific cases non-overridable by human approval. This
ADR now covers both the original display question and the correctness
issue underneath it.

## Options considered (retained for record)

**A. Binary, decision-outcome-based display** — `HIGH` if `ALLOW`, `LOW`
if `REQUIRE_APPROVAL`. **Defect identified in review: ignores `DENY`
entirely**, which risks a confidence-display abstraction implying a hard
denial is merely "low confidence" — it is not the same thing.

**B. Rule-margin-based display** — deferred pending a `PolicyRule` schema
change (unchanged reasoning from Revision 1; still the long-term direction,
still not resolved here).

## Decision (Revision 2)

**MVP policy outcome is `ALLOW | REQUIRE_APPROVAL | DENY`.** Policy-certainty
display is **categorical**, derived directly from this three-valued
deterministic outcome — never a numeric score, and never something that can
obscure a `DENY` as if it were merely "low confidence." No numeric margin is
presented until margin-capable rules and calibration exist (Option B's
timing, unchanged).

**Fail-closed rule:** if policy evaluation itself fails — exception,
timeout, malformed rule, missing required input — the outcome is **`DENY`**,
never `REQUIRE_APPROVAL`. A failed evaluation is not "let a human decide,"
it's "the engine could not determine authorization, so none exists."

**Hard-deny category:** some `DENY` outcomes are absolute and **not
overridable by any human approval**, regardless of role or risk tolerance:

- unregistered action (no matching `ActionDefinition`)
- tenant mismatch (resolved tenant doesn't match the request's tenant —
  NFR-04's invariant enforced at the policy boundary, not only the
  repository layer)
- expired authorization
- prohibited environment (`environment_constraints` doesn't include the
  target environment)
- missing required evidence (an OPC's `required_context` marked
  `required: true` is absent)
- kill switch active (§7A — a halted execution or tenant cannot be
  re-authorized around the halt)
- immutable policy constraint violation (a rule the tenant has marked
  non-overridable in policy config)

A `REQUIRE_APPROVAL` outcome can be resolved by a human. A hard `DENY`
cannot — it requires fixing the underlying condition and re-submitting, not
an override.

## Rationale

This is not a display nicety — it's a correctness defect in the one
component whose entire value proposition is deterministic authority
(NFR-05). Failing open on evaluation error, or letting a confidence-display
abstraction imply `DENY` is just "very low confidence `ALLOW`," both
quietly weaken the actual authorization boundary. The hard-deny list makes
explicit what was previously only implicit in scattered spec sections
(§7A's halt behavior, §23's tenant invariant, §14A's expiry) — collecting
them here gives the Policy Engine one authoritative checklist to enforce,
not five scattered mentions to remember to wire up.

## Consequences

- **(+)** `DENY` remains a real, first-class, fail-closed outcome — never silently reinterpreted as a display concern.
- **(+)** The hard-deny list gives LLD/Build a single, complete checklist instead of discovering conditions piecemeal.
- **(+)** Categorical display is honest about what's actually known, consistent with ADR-003 and ADR-007's false-precision corrections.
- **(–)** The Policy Engine's evaluation-failure path now needs explicit, tested fail-closed behavior — a real implementation requirement; a policy bug that previously might have degraded to "ask a human" must now be caught and correctly resolve to `DENY`.
- **(–)** The hard-deny list must be enforced *before* any approval-routing logic runs, not after — an ordering constraint on the Policy Engine's internal evaluation flow that LLD must make explicit.
